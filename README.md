# From Target to Patient

### Causal Methods Across Therapeutic Discovery and Development

Drug discovery and development is a chain of causal questions, for therapeutics of any modality. Following Daphne Koller's framing, the chain has three links, and each can fail:

1. **Mechanism → target.** Is this target a causal driver of disease, or a bystander? A literature-derived mechanism model, with every edge graded by the kind of evidence behind it, is tested using human genetics (Mendelian randomization, colocalization).
2. **Target → therapeutic.** Does intervening on the target produce the intended effect? Genetic variants that mimic lifelong target perturbation serve as natural experiments, and their predictions are benchmarked against what randomized trials later showed for approved and failed therapeutics.
3. **Therapeutic → patient.** Which patients' disease runs through that mechanism, and who will respond? A patient model of clinical phenotypes and endotypes, and the mapping between mechanism and patient, are tested against synovial tissue cohorts and published trial results stratified by patient subgroup.
